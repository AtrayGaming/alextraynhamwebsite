Academic sponsorship proposal · 2025

Villains Media Night

A sponsorship proposal for a one-night Disney Villains media event at Walt Disney World Resort. It lays out the night, the audience in the room, what a sponsor would receive, and the deadlines that would put their brand on site.

Academic proposal. I wrote this proposal as an academic project in 2025. The event was not produced, and no sponsor signed on. The brands pictured in the proposal are examples of the kinds of partners it was written for, not sponsors.

My role
Author, and the Sponsorships Lead the proposal names as the sponsor contact
The event
One night, November 7, 2025, at Walt Disney World Resort
The audience
Content creators, influencers, press, and VIP guests
The document
A five-page proposal: event overview, sponsor benefits, featured partner examples, and a deliverable timeline

01The opportunity

A night built around the villains.

Disney Villains Media Night is a one-night celebration built around some of the most recognizable characters in Disney history. The proposal describes live entertainment, themed dining, interactive sets, and rare character appearances, in a room limited to invited guests.

The audience is the point. The room holds content creators, influencers, press representatives, and VIP guests, and collectively those attendees reach large, engaged followings. Every activation, every shared post, and every highlight from the night travels far beyond the event space, which is what makes the night worth a sponsor's attention.

02What sponsors would get

Visibility that is part of the night, not next to it.

The proposal states the offer in three parts. A sponsor gains national exposure across traditional and digital media channels reaching Disney fans. It aligns with the Disney Villains brand, which the proposal describes as recognized for bold storytelling and cultural relevance. And it amplifies visibility through the social coverage of the invited influencers and press.

The integrations are physical, not just logos on a step and repeat. The proposal names branded lounges, product sampling, and themed activations as the ways in, so that when guests share their experiences, sponsor brands are woven into the story of the night rather than placed beside it.

To show the level of visibility on offer, the proposal pictures example partner brands: AdventHealth, Coca-Cola, Dole, Funko, Ghirardelli, and AT&T. It says plainly that these are examples of the types of brands whose presence fits the tone and audience of the event, and that actual sponsors would receive the same level of visibility and brand integration.

03The timeline

Six dates between commitment and the event.

The proposal closes with the part most sponsorship documents leave vague: what is due, and when. Each deadline comes with a plain statement of what it means for the sponsor.

Sponsor commitment · October 1, 2025

Confirm participation to reserve activation space and inclusion in marketing planning.

Artwork and branding · October 10, 2025

Provide vector logos and creative assets for invitations, graphics, and promotional videos.

Payment processing · October 15, 2025

Complete payment to secure production, staffing, and placement.

Video promo integration · October 17 to 31, 2025

Approve placement and talking points for inclusion in promotional content.

Final event preparation · November 6, 2025

Confirm deliverables, shipping details, and on-site needs so the brand appears on signage, decor, and attendee kits.

Event date · November 7, 2025

Activate on site with staff ready for guest engagement and content capture.

The proposal is direct about why the dates matter: missing one could limit a brand's visibility in the marketing moments that carry the most weight.

04What it demonstrates

Connecting a creative idea to what a partner receives.

The sponsor's side of the page. My part of this project was the sponsor-facing document itself. It is written from the partner's seat: what the night is, who is in the room, what their brand gets, and what they have to hand over and by when. An event idea is only a sponsorship opportunity once someone can read it that way.

Benefits stated as reach, not adjectives. The proposal describes the audience by who they are and what their coverage does, instead of attaching an attendance figure it does not have. The claim it makes for the event is the one the format can support: a small room with a long reach.

Deadlines as part of the offer. The timeline is not an appendix. It is how the proposal promises that an activation ends up in the invitations, the signage, and the attendee kits instead of being promised for them. That is the same discipline the rest of my work runs on: planned ahead, and clear on first read.
