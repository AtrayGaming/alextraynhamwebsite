One Group,
Different Speeds.

A YouTube concept that helps friends with different ride preferences picture a day in Celestial Park together.

Hypothetical portfolio project. Not commissioned, approved, or endorsed by Universal Orlando Resort. This concept has not been filmed or launched. All performance targets are proposed.

My role
Audience strategy, concept, script, storyboard, packaging, evaluation plan
Audience
Adults planning a first Epic Universe visit with a partner or friends
Format
One five-minute YouTube episode + two vertical edits
Stage
Written concept & editorial prototype · October 2026

01The brief

A shared trip. Different ideas of a good day.

One person wants a coaster. Another wants time to explore. The planner needs to see how both preferences can fit into the same visit.

“Will everyone have a good day, or will someone spend it waiting around?”

The opportunity is to answer that planning question through a story: two friends choose something for themselves, then reunite for a shared moment. The viewer leaves with one practical idea: one choice each, one moment together.

Audience hypothesis

Showing both friends enjoying their choices could make a mixed-preference visit easier to imagine than a list of attractions alone. This is a hypothesis to test, not an established guest finding.

Business objective

Support destination consideration and encourage a next step into official planning content. Guide visits would be a useful signal; they would not prove incremental ticket sales.

Why this setting?

Celestial Park combines rides with gardens, fountains, and dining. That range provides a grounded setting for the story. My Carousel experience informs the questions I ask about clarity, while the concept itself uses public information.

Editorial positioning

Official destination guides explain the available experiences. This episode would organize them around a group decision. It is a focused editorial angle, not a claim that Universal has never addressed this need.

Start with the people, then show the park.

Original sample · Video description

Planning Epic Universe with people who enjoy different levels of thrills? Follow two friends through Celestial Park as they choose their own experiences and make time for a shared stop. Explore your options in the official Celestial Park guide linked below.

Creative guardrails

Both friends get an active story. No pressure to ride, promises about waits, or assumptions about medical suitability. The edit must not imply the two activities take equal time. An independent prototype can use narration and permitted public-area exterior footage; it does not need backstage access or internal procedures.

03Distribution & production

One production, three useful outputs.

Proposed deliverables and channel roles
Deliverable Treatment Purpose
YouTube · ~5 minutes Full choice-and-reunion story with captions and one guide link Help an interested planner picture a workable visit
Shorts / Reels · 25–35 seconds Opening exchange, then a glimpse of both friends enjoying their choices Introduce the audience problem; point to the full story using available platform links
Shorts / Reels · 20–30 seconds One choice each, one shared moment, explained as a visual recap Deliver a standalone planning takeaway worth saving or sharing

A small, practical production

Two adult participants, one compact camera setup, public-area coverage, and separately recorded voiceover where needed. Frame key moments for vertical reuse. Track filming, editing, captions, and revision hours to understand the real effort per usable output.

Keep changing prices and live wait times out of the core story. Check public attraction details before production and link to current official planning information. This gives the episode a longer useful life without promising that every detail will remain unchanged.

04Measurement

Measure the promise, the attention, and the next step.

These are proposed pilot thresholds, not Universal benchmarks or predicted results. Relevant channel baselines would be needed before an official launch.

Evaluation plan · No campaign data exists
Question Proposed criterion Access / actual result
Is the idea clear? 4 of 5 prototype viewers can describe two options and one shared moment Small usability check · Not tested
Does the opening deliver? At least 70% still watching at 0:30 YouTube Studio · Not launched
Does the story hold attention? At least 45% average percentage viewed, or 2:15 of a five-minute cut YouTube Studio · Not launched
Does it support planning? 10 attributed guide sessions per 1,000 video views within 28 days Approved tagging + destination analytics · Unavailable
Is production efficient? 1 main episode + 2 usable vertical edits; record hours and cost per output Production log · Not produced

Guide sessions per 1,000 views is a normalized traffic indicator, not a person-level conversion rate. A tagged Universal link would not give me access to Universal's analytics. An outbound click on this portfolio would be a different metric.

Review, learn, revise

  • Before filming: test a narrated storyboard with five intended viewers and record confusion in their own words.
  • Day 7: review retention, traffic sources, and comments for a mismatch between the click promise and the opening.
  • Day 28: compare with similar-length planning videos on the same channel, separate paid reach, and review the fixed measurement window.
  • Small sample: below 1,000 views, describe rates as directional. This is a practical review floor, not a significance test.

If people leave before the choices are clear, investigate the opening. If the exploration segment loses attention, give that friend a stronger discovery. If retention is healthy but guide traffic is weak, test the call to action. Each is a hypothesis, not proof of the cause.
