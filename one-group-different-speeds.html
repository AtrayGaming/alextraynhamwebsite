One Group,
Different Speeds.

A YouTube concept that helps friends with different ride preferences picture a day in Celestial Park together.

Hypothetical portfolio project. Not commissioned, approved, or endorsed by Universal Orlando Resort. This concept has not been filmed or launched. All performance targets are proposed.

My role
Audience strategy, concept, script, storyboard, packaging, evaluation plan
Audience
The planner in a mixed-thrill group: the person making a first Epic Universe visit work for everyone
Format
One five-minute YouTube episode + two vertical edits
Stage
Written concept & editorial prototype · October 2026

What this demonstrates: audience strategy, video packaging, lean production design, and measurement planning.

01The brief

One trip, different ideas of a good day.

One person wants a coaster. Another wants time to explore. The planner needs to see how both preferences can fit into the same visit.

“Will everyone have a good day, or will someone spend it waiting around?”

The opportunity is to answer that planning question through a story: two friends choose something for themselves, then reunite for a shared moment. The viewer leaves with one practical idea: one choice each, one moment together.

Audience hypothesis

Showing both friends enjoying their choices could make a mixed-preference visit easier to imagine than a list of attractions alone. This is a hypothesis to test, not an established guest finding.

Business objective

Support destination consideration and encourage a next step into official planning content. Guide visits would be a useful signal; they would not prove incremental ticket sales.

Why a story, not a list?

Official planning content already lists every attraction, show, and restaurant. A planner with a mixed-thrill group does not need a longer list; they need a workable way to decide. This format gives them one decision framework, shown as a story they can picture their own group in.

Why this setting?

Celestial Park combines rides with gardens, fountains, and dining. It is also the hub every other world branches off and returns to, so splitting up and reuniting there is how a visit to Epic Universe naturally works. That range provides a grounded setting for the story. Working at the Constellation Carousel, I watch groups negotiate different thrill levels every day, and this format is built around that negotiation. The concept itself uses public information.

02Creative samples

Start with the people, then show the park.

Working title: Celestial Park for a Mixed-Thrill Group | Epic Universe

Logline: Two friends with different ride preferences each pick their own Celestial Park ride, then reunite at the fountains for one moment together.

Original sample · Opening script

Friend A“I came for the coaster.”

Friend B“I came with you. Those are different things.”

Voiceover“Planning Epic Universe with people who like different levels of thrills? Let's try Celestial Park. We'll each choose something we want to do, then meet up for one shared stop. By the end, you'll have a starting point for your own group.”

Friend B“You get the launch. I get the stars.”

On-screen line: One choice each. One moment together.

Six-beat editorial storyboard

A text storyboard for the proposed five-minute episode. The timings describe an edit, not real attraction or walking times.

010:00 to 0:25

Two-shot + reactions

The difference

Two friends introduce different preferences. Establish the planning question immediately.

020:25 to 0:55

Graphic + wide shot

A simple plan

Name one choice for each person and one shared meeting point. Keep the geography understandable.

030:55 to 1:45

Exterior + rider reactions

Friend B's choice: the Constellation Carousel

Friend B picks the Carousel and says why it is their speed: it lifts and turns among the constellations. A real ride with its own payoff, not a waiting spot. The gardens and fountains play as setting here, not as the choice itself.

041:45 to 3:00

Intercut sequences

Friend A's choice: Stardust Racers

Friend A takes on Stardust Racers, the dual-launch racing coaster. Intercut the two rides so both friends are mid-experience at the same time, and each returns with a story to tell.

053:00 to 4:10

Reunion + close-ups

Come back together

Meet at a preselected public spot: the fountains by the Chronos tower, the centerpiece of Celestial Park. Each friend shares a favorite discovery from their choice.

064:10 to 5:00

Recap graphic + end card

Make it usable

Recap the two choices and the shared moment, and direct the viewer to the official Celestial Park planning guide.

Thumbnail layout studies

Keep the title fixed and test whether people or experiences communicate the promise more clearly. These are layout wireframes; original or cleared photography still needs to be produced.

Original sample · Video description

Planning Epic Universe with people who enjoy different levels of thrills? Follow two friends through Celestial Park as each picks a ride at their own speed and they make time for one shared stop. Explore your options in the official Celestial Park guide linked below.

Creative guardrails

Both friends get an active story. No pressure to ride, promises about waits, or assumptions about medical suitability, and the edit does not imply the two activities take equal time.

03Distribution & production

One production day covers all three outputs.

Proposed deliverables and channel roles
Deliverable Treatment Purpose
YouTube · ~5 minutes Full choice-and-reunion story with captions and one guide link Help an interested planner picture a workable visit
Shorts / Reels · 25 to 35 seconds Opening exchange, then a glimpse of both friends enjoying their choices Introduce the audience problem; point to the full story using available platform links
Shorts / Reels · 20 to 30 seconds One choice each, one shared moment, explained as a visual recap Deliver a standalone planning takeaway worth saving or sharing

A small, practical production

Two adult participants, one compact camera setup, public-area coverage, and separately recorded voiceover where needed. Frame key moments for vertical reuse. Track filming, editing, captions, and revision hours to understand the real effort per usable output.

Changing prices and live wait times stay out of the core story; public attraction details get checked before production, with a link to current official planning information.

04Measurement

What I would measure.

Proposed pilot thresholds, not benchmarks or predictions. Channel baselines would be set before any launch. Status for every row is in the last column; no campaign data exists yet.

Evaluation plan · No campaign data exists
Question Proposed criterion Access / actual result
Is the idea clear? 4 of 5 prototype viewers can describe two options and one shared moment Not tested (usability check)
Does the opening deliver? At least 70% still watching at 0:30 Not launched (YouTube Studio)
Does the story hold attention? At least 45% average percentage viewed, or 2:15 of a five-minute cut Not launched (YouTube Studio)
Does it support planning? 10 attributed guide sessions per 1,000 video views within 28 days Unavailable (needs approved tagging)

Guide sessions per 1,000 views is a normalized traffic indicator, not a person-level conversion rate. A tagged Universal link would not give me access to Universal's analytics. An outbound click on this portfolio would be a different metric.

If people leave before the choices are clear, investigate the opening. If the exploration segment loses attention, give that friend a stronger discovery. If retention is healthy but guide traffic is weak, test the call to action. Each is a hypothesis, not proof of the cause.

05Reflection & next step

The strategy needs to become something people can react to.

This concept connects my experience explaining unfamiliar attractions with the creator decisions I enjoy: the hook, the packaging, the edit, and what to learn from the response. Underneath it is something I see at the Carousel every shift: in most groups, one person quietly worries they are the reason everyone else has to slow down. This format is built so nobody has to be the bottleneck.
